# Handoff brief

Written for: the helper (an AI tool, an assistant, a service or a person).
Use it when: you are handing over a task that will take more than a few minutes, or that you will not be watching.

> Guidance lines start with `>`. Delete them before you send. Fill in what applies and delete the rest.
> Test before sending: read the brief as a stranger with none of your history. Could they start?

## 1. The task at a glance

- Task name: ____
- Date handed over: ____
- From: ____
- To (helper, tool or service): ____
- Needed by: ____ (date, time and time zone)
- Priority: [ ] must land by the deadline  [ ] preferably by the deadline  [ ] no fixed date

## 2. Goal

> One sentence on what you want, one on what it is for. The reason lets the helper make sensible choices you did not think of.

- I want: ____
- So that: ____
- The decision or action this feeds: ____

## 3. Context

> Include what the helper cannot guess: the audience, the history, the words you use in a special way.

- Background, in two or three sentences: ____
- Who will read or use the result: ____
- What has been tried before, and what happened: ____
- Terms that mean something specific here:
  - ____ means ____
  - ____ means ____

## 4. Inputs

> List everything the helper should read or use, and where it lives. Say which version is final.

| Input | Where it is | Final or draft | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |

One good example, if you have one:

- Example: ____
- Copy from it: ____ (structure, length, order of points, tone)
- Change from it: ____ (names, dates, numbers, anything that belonged to the old situation)
- Not like this: ____ because ____

> Strip private or one-off details out of examples and swap in placeholders in square brackets.

## 5. Constraints

> Separate what is fixed from what is open. A helper with no open choices cannot help. A helper with no fixed ones will guess.

Fixed (do not change):

- ____
- ____

Open (use your judgment, and tell me what you chose):

- ____

Out of scope (do not do, even if it looks helpful):

- ____

Limits:

- Time or effort to spend: ____
- Spending allowed, if any: ____ (write none if none)
- Sources it may use: ____
- Sources it must not use: ____

## 6. Output shape

> Ask for the shape you will actually use. A table you must retype is not the same as a table you can paste.

- Format: ____ (for example a short email, a table, a list, a document, a set of files)
- Length: ____
- Structure or headings: ____
- Language and tone: ____
- Dates, numbers and units written as: ____
- File name pattern: ____
- Deliver to (place or channel): ____
- Put first: ____ (the answer, the decision needed, or the main risk)
- Include at the end: [ ] sources  [ ] assumptions made  [ ] anything you could not do  [ ] questions for me

## 7. Definition of done

> Write this before work starts. definition-of-done.md has a fuller version.

Done means all of these are true:

- [ ] ____
- [ ] ____
- [ ] ____

Not required (so nobody polishes the wrong thing): ____

I will accept it by checking: ____

## 8. Check-ins

> Fixed windows beat constant pinging. Decide when you will look and what you want to see.

- First progress report by: ____ (date, time and time zone)
- Then every: ____
- Each report says: what is done, what is next, what is blocked, what you need from me
- I will read reports at: ____ (for example 10:00 and 16:00 on working days)
- Reach me about urgent matters by: ____
- Between windows, make the decisions this brief allows, and note what you chose

## 9. Stop conditions

Stop and ask me, instead of continuing, if:

- [ ] an input is missing, empty, or looks different from what this brief describes
- [ ] the work needs something listed as out of scope or as never to touch
- [ ] the effort or spending limit in section 5 is about to be passed
- [ ] two instructions in this brief conflict
- [ ] you are about to do something that cannot be undone
- [ ] ____

When you stop, tell me what you did, what you found, what you would do next, and the question you need answered.

## 10. Permissions

> Grant access, not secrets. Never paste passwords, keys or codes into this brief. permission-and-secrets-checklist.md covers it.

May do on its own:

- ____

May suggest, and I decide:

- ____

Must never touch:

- ____

Access granted for this task: ____ (names of accounts, folders and tools; level; until when)

## 11. Before you start

- Ask me up to ____ questions now, in one message, if anything above is unclear.
- If I have not answered by ____, go ahead on these assumptions and list them in your report: ____

## A filled example (illustrative)

> A made-up brief, to show the level of detail. Not a recommendation about any topic.

- I want: a one-page comparison of three quotes for office chairs.
- So that: I can choose one at Thursday's planning meeting.
- Fixed: the three quotes in the shared folder. Open: how to lay out the comparison.
- Out of scope: contacting any supplier.
- Output: a table first, then three sentences on the main risk of each option, with sources listed at the end.
- Done means: every figure in the table matches a quote, and anything unclear is listed under "questions".
- Stop and ask if: a quote is missing a price or a delivery date.
